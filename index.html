01 / About

Scholarship grounded in questions of participation and possibility.

I am a tenured Associate Professor of Special Education and Educational Specialist Program Coordinator in the Department of Education at Santa Clara University. I earned my Ph.D. in Special Education from the University of Wisconsin–Madison, following degrees in elementary education from Seoul National University of Education. My research examines racial disproportionality in disability identification and school discipline, critical disability studies in education, culturally sustaining Universal Design for Learning, research–practice partnerships, and inclusive teacher education.

Across research, teaching, and collaboration, I am interested in how theory can help us see familiar educational problems differently—and imagine alternative ways of organizing educational life.

02 / Research

Lines of inquiry

Interconnected questions that guide my scholarship.

01

Structural inequities in special education

Examining how race, disability, language, and institutional practices intersect in educational identification, placement, and access.

02

Collective agency and educational transformation

Exploring how educators, students, families, and communities work across boundaries to reimagine and transform educational practices.

03

Disability, difference, and theoretical inquiry

Drawing on cultural-historical and sociomaterial perspectives to reconsider participation, agency, and the production of difference.

04 / Teaching

Teaching as inquiry and collective learning.

My teaching connects educational theory with the questions, tensions, and possibilities educators encounter in practice. I seek to create spaces for critical reflection, collaborative inquiry, and the development of more inclusive educational arrangements.

Research and teaching interests include inclusive teacher education, culturally sustaining Universal Design for Learning, and research–practice partnerships.

05 / Engagement

Research in conversation with practice.

My scholarly and professional engagements connect research, educator learning, and collaborative efforts to advance educational inclusion and equity.

Current work includes the Warmenhoven Collaborative Leadership Academy for Transformative Leadership and a 2026–2027 funded participatory study of arts-based youth advocacy with youth of color with disabilities from immigrant-origin communities.
